Jieun Kim

Investigating Trustworthy Machine Learning, Model Reliability & Robustness, Representation Learning, and Brain-Inspired AI

I am a Machine Learning Researcher focusing on Trustworthy and Brain-Inspired AI, spanning explainable time-series anomaly detection, continuous-time neural representation, and robust computational dynamics. My research background includes modeling Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) at the Korea Institute of Science and Technology (KIST) advised by Dr. Taegon Kim, developing unsupervised Neural ODE frameworks for irregular telemetry at the Georgia Institute of Technology (Atlanta, GA, USA), and engineering distribution-guided Transformers for missing value imputation during my Master's at Korea University. Across these domains, my goal is to build reliable, explainable, and physics-guided AI systems for real-world decision making.

I received my M.S. in Industrial and Management Engineering from Korea University advised by Prof. Jun-Geol Baek, and hold a B.S. in Statistics & Computer Science from Sookmyung Women's University advised by Prof. Young-Geun Choi.

Korea Institute of Science and Technology (KIST)

Research Intern | Advisor: Dr. Taegon Kim

May 2024 – Feb 2026

Research Focus & Objectives

Investigating topographic cortico-basal ganglia computational circuits to computationally model the emergence of both motor and non-motor symptom comorbidities in Parkinson’s disease (PD). The overarching goal is to understand how altered spiking dynamics and network-level synchronization degrade signal processing in neural systems.

Technical & Methodology

  • Constructed large-scale Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) incorporating realistic anatomical connectivity, topographic input projections, and synaptic plasticity parameters.
  • Quantitative simulation of population spiking activity and phase-locking neural synchronization across baseline vs. dopamine-depleted pathological states.
  • Extracted dynamic biomarkers to optimize predictive algorithms and metrics for adaptive closed-loop Deep Brain Stimulation (aDBS).

Georgia Institute of Technology (Atlanta, GA, USA)

Visiting Researcher | Advisor: Prof. Jungho Lewe

Aug 2023 – Feb 2024

Unsupervised Neural ODE Modeling

Constructed an unsupervised anomaly detection framework for unlabeled non-continuous sensor telemetry without artificial imputation, deploying Neural ODEs (continuous-time differential equations) to preserve 100% raw data dynamics while detecting point anomaly spikes.

Technical & Physical Feature Engineering

  • Physical Feature Derived Equations:
    • Supply/Return Temperature Difference: $\Delta T_t = T_{\text{return}, t} - T_{\text{supply}, t}$
    • Heat Flow Ratio: $\text{Heat Flow Ratio}_t = \frac{\Delta T_t}{\text{Flow}_t} = \frac{T_{\text{return}, t} - T_{\text{supply}, t}}{\text{Flow}_t}$
  • Neural ODEs & SRE Reconstruction Scoring: Continuous-time differential parameterization to model raw sensor trajectories directly without forced interpolation artifacts. SRE Scoring spike detection (Threshold > 3.8).

Industrial Research Projects

Multiple Time-series Anomaly Detection & Predictive Maintenance Systems

Project 01

Hyundai Motor Group: Multiple Time-series Anomaly Detection

Hydrogen Fuel Cell Vehicle (FCEV) Pre-failure Anomaly Detection (2021.07 – 2022.06)

2021.07 ~ 2022.06

Project Overview

  • Goal: Develop a real-time sensor-based pre-failure anomaly detection model for Hydrogen Fuel Cell Vehicles (FCEVs).
  • Data Unit: 386,842 total observations collected per single driving cycle (`Driving_Cycle_ID`, 99.8% normal vs. 0.2% abnormal).
  • Modeling & XAI: One-Class Autoencoder trained on normal driving dynamics + SHAP feature attribution to isolate root cause sensors ($X_1 \sim X_5$).
Project 02

BisTelligence: Industrial Pump RUL & Health Index System

Industrial Pump Remaining Useful Life (RUL) & Monitoring (2022.06 – 2023.01)

2022.06 ~ 2023.01

Project Overview

  • Goal: Construct a sensor-driven Remaining Useful Life (RUL) prediction and predictive maintenance system for industrial pumps.
  • Data Feature: Variable sequence lengths per pump (1,100 to 1,850 steps) due to varying load operating lifetimes.
  • Modeling: LSTM-Autoencoder metric calculating Health Index (HI) trained on initial 30% normal baseline.
