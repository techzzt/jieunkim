Jieun Kim

Investigating Trustworthy Machine Learning, Model Reliability & Robustness, Representation Learning, and Brain-Inspired AI

I am a Machine Learning Researcher focusing on Trustworthy and Brain-Inspired AI, spanning explainable time-series anomaly detection, continuous-time neural representation, and robust computational dynamics. My research background includes modeling Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) at the Korea Institute of Science and Technology (KIST) advised by Dr. Taegon Kim, developing unsupervised Neural ODE frameworks for irregular telemetry at the Georgia Institute of Technology, and engineering distribution-guided Transformers for missing value imputation during my Master's at Korea University. Across these domains, my goal is to build reliable, explainable, and physics-guided AI systems for real-world decision making.

I received my M.S. in Industrial and Management Engineering from Korea University advised by Prof. Jun-Geol Baek, and hold a B.S. in Statistics & Computer Science from Sookmyung Women's University advised by Prof. Young-Geun Choi.

Research Focus & Objectives

Investigating topographic cortico-basal ganglia computational circuits to model the emergence of motor and non-motor symptom comorbidities in Parkinson’s disease (PD). Technical & Methodology

Georgia Institute of Technology

Visiting Researcher · Advisor: Prof. Jungho Lewe

Aug 2023 – Feb 2024

Unsupervised Neural ODE Modeling

Developed an unsupervised anomaly detection framework for unlabeled, irregularly sampled sensor telemetry. Implemented continuous-time Neural ODEs to directly parameterize raw signal dynamics without artificial imputation artifacts.

Technical & Physical Feature Engineering

  • Physical Feature Formulation:
    • Temp Difference: $\Delta T_t = T_{\text{return}, t} - T_{\text{supply}, t}$
    • Heat Flow Ratio: $\text{Heat Flow Ratio}_t = \frac{\Delta T_t}{\text{Flow}_t}$
  • Continuous Dynamics & SRE Scoring: Differential parameterization capturing non-stationary sensor trajectories directly, paired with Sub-sequence Reconstruction Error (SRE) scoring for spike anomaly detection (Threshold > 3.8).

Projects

Real-time anomaly detection, model explainability (XAI), and predictive maintenance applications

Project 01

Hyundai Motor Group: Sensor Anomaly Detection

Fuel Cell Vehicle (FCEV) Pre-failure Anomaly Detection & Feature Attribution (2021.07 – 2022.06)

2021.07 ~ 2022.06

Project Overview

  • Goal: Formulate a real-time sensor-based anomaly detection framework for Hydrogen Fuel Cell Vehicles (FCEVs).
  • Data Scope: 386,842 driving-cycle observations (`Driving_Cycle_ID`, 99.8% normal vs. 0.2% abnormal).
  • Modeling & XAI: Trained a One-Class Autoencoder on baseline driving dynamics and integrated SHAP feature attribution to isolate root-cause sensor variables ($X_1 \sim X_5$).
One-Class Autoencoder SHAP Feature Attribution Anomaly Detection Industrial Telemetry
Project 02

BisTelligence: Industrial Pump RUL & Health Index System

Industrial Pump Remaining Useful Life (RUL) & Condition Monitoring (2022.06 – 2023.01)

2022.06 ~ 2023.01

Project Overview

  • Goal: Build a sensor-driven Remaining Useful Life (RUL) estimation and predictive maintenance system for industrial pumps.
  • Data Characteristics: Accommodated variable sequence lengths (1,100 to 1,850 time steps) resulting from varying operational run-to-failure cycles.
  • Modeling: Formulated an LSTM-Autoencoder Health Index (HI) metric calibrated on initial 30% baseline normal operations.
LSTM-Autoencoder Health Index (HI) Predictive Maintenance RUL Estimation
